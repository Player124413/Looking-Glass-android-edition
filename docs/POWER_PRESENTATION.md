# Power-up and first-person presentation

Rage uses the ordered `RAGE_*` legs sequence in `global/alice_torso.st`: ready, infection start, two pants, rise, grow, stand and two ready poses. Tea uses `grasshoppertea` / `hopp.ska`. Inventory owns damage, jumping, mutual exclusion, difficulty scaling and duration. Ordinary power pickups retain an interruptible presentation layer. The fortress2 pickup instead uses its existing map owner for the full 12.5-second Rage performance: input and attacks are locked, original full-body clips and camera endpoints share the saved scene clock, and machinery still carries Alice. Pause freezes the scene; watch and hold-Enter skip share the supported gameplay handoff. Completed/older callbacks do not replay on load.

The Rage camera uses the reviewed player-relative eye/target triples and a five-unit collision sweep. Exponential settling is a deterministic port approximation; the original DLL's exact timestep normalization remains unresolved. This is a player-state camera, not a borrowed map track. Native SKAN durations are checked against the scene timeline at load. Watching and skipping release the lift only after completing the appearance; the normal mover carries Alice down 48 units over four seconds. Neither path refreshes the power timer or advances the guard callback clock. Old saves without the optional lift phase retain their existing movement without rewinding the platform. The right HUD foldout uses the original Rage, Tea or Glass icon for the currently active effect, including restored/carry-over timers.

Face/arm textures are original shader slots: Rage face 3 / arms 2 and Tea face 2 / arms 1. The supplied gameplay DLL confirms a 2.5-second skin delay, Rage attachments on back/head/left forearm, Tea wings on back and headgear on head. Growth is presentation only. Rage hides the replaced ordinary hair and left lower-arm surface; the right hand remains intact. The opaque `rage_arm` skin retains its original RGB with full coverage: stored zero alpha over its fingers must not become a generic cutout. Expiry/death removes attachments and restores all surfaces. One scoped mesh mask is restored after each draw. The implementation does not enable the commented-out rage-expulsion clip or execute arbitrary source script events.

The collected fortress vial now retains its opening TAN and four animated vent anchors. Red gas starts at frame 39 (1.95 seconds); the vial fades after opening. The body and attachments use the supplied `powerups/rage` red reflection/bulge layer, with a short onset and expiry fade. The vent birth positions derive from the saved scene clock, so paused and restored views reconstruct the same particles. Completed or skipped scenes remove the used vial without creating another collectible.

All eight Rage clip joins blend over at most 0.15 seconds without lengthening the 12.5-second sequence. Alternate skins blend in after the verified 2.5-second delay; attachment growth eases and the final pose releases through the existing blend. These interpolation ramps and the used-vial fade are presentation approximations. Native dynamic-light radius/style, exact used-vial resize and the separate gas helper's caller timing remain unverified; they are not claimed as complete.

The archive audit found no separate first-person hand rig or dedicated first-person animations. At the user's request, first-person gameplay displays only the equipped toy and its effects. It retains the reconstructed weapon placements, walking bob, attack motions and equip transitions. The authoritative action/resource clock, eye aiming and wall collision sweeps are unchanged. The fortress Rage scene shows Alice with its own camera in either view preference, then returns to that preference. Existing saves, including interim saves with arm-placement fields, remain readable.

Run `--presentation-check` for source-asset/state checks and `--presentation-render` for native power and first-person render/save fixtures. Captures and local research remain in `private/`, excluded from source distribution.
