# Beyond the Wall

Launch **tools/launchers/Launch-Beyond.cmd** for a fresh visit, or choose **Beyond the Wall** in the Tab chapter menu. The campaign entrance follows the first Fortress of Doors visit. **E** opens the ordinary hallway doors and operates nearby levers. **Shift + Space** performs a running jump.

The level controller restores the flipping corridor, the musical door puzzle and reset lever, the rolling three-piece walkway, the rising arch room and waving staircase, and the shuffled return doors. Drawing, collision and attached triggers use the same machinery positions. The original three musical notes and two Cheshire hints are read from the user's archives.

Listen to the three notes before descending to the levers. The upper lever resets a failed attempt and plays the notes again. After solving the puzzle, climb the spiral staircase back up. Follow the rolling walkway, jump the gap at its far end, and enter the portal. In the arch room, wait for the reconstruction and time jumps as the destination steps descend. Watch the initially open door during the final shuffle.

The note demonstration, reset replay, successful solve and arch-room reveal now use their authored camera tracks (the reset uses its placed camera node). The doors and their three notes follow the scene timing, and solved levers disappear behind the final fade. Hold Enter, or the displayed controller button, to skip. The arch reveal uses its declared wide lens.

Watching, skipping and loading preserve earned progress. Skipping the arch camera leaves the room rising on its existing clock; it does not solve the levers, finish the walkway or open the final exit. Alice stays at her supported position and receives control with no leftover velocity. A save made during a scene resumes it; a previously completed puzzle in an older save stays completed without replaying the scene.

Machinery clocks, lever progress, camera playback, walkway direction and final-door retries are saved. Older generic `fortress2` saves gain the new controller and restart at its entrance while retaining inventory and resources. Other visited maps remain in the campaign save.

The optional Rage Box starts the original nine-pose transformation, with player-relative camera endpoints, collision clearance and locked input. Alice remains visible in either view preference. The lift stays raised throughout the performance, then lowers 48 units over four seconds after watching or skipping. Existing saves keep any descent already in progress. Its saved presentation clock, safe handoff and Will-side power icon are described in `POWER_PRESENTATION.md`. Existing consumed pickups do not replay.

This restores the main gameplay sequence; it is not a complete recreation of the original presentation. Cheshire hint camera staging, exact Rage camera damping and used-box vents, background flying-prop paths, some bound decorative props and effects, and exact enemy staging remain unfinished. The common camera handoff/fades remain port presentation; the original solve camera blend and fake-player gestures are not reproduced exactly. Combat uses the project's existing supported enemy behaviours. The original game data stays local and is never included in source packages.

### The route is driven from the level's state and from what Alice has left

The route was first recorded from a full Sanity and Will bar with fixed waits (`r.wait(n)`), and it did not survive the campaign chain: the chain hands Alice over with what the visits before took from her (Normal: 57 Sanity and 22 Will, after the slime in Pandemonium and 26 Cards in the first Fortress visit; Easy: 78 and 65). With that Will the Cards ran out in the first hall, the two Boojums that the recorded route killed with Cards on the stairs lived on into the rolling walkway, their screams' shove and their hits dropped Alice off it (the pit's recovery portal, then the basement, where she died), and the fixed waits met the machinery in the wrong phase (on Easy the walk along the corridor simply failed, as its platforms are not there). No gameplay value was changed. The driver (`src/beyond_route.rs`) now does two things:

- **It waits on the machinery's own clocks.** `Beyond::long_phase`, `arch_time`, `sliding` and `last_ready` (read-only accessors) tell it what the level is doing. The flipping corridor is crossed by its 88.5 s cycle (each move waits for the phase the recorded route made it at), the arch room by the arch clock (the room is up at 18.4 s, and the steps swing on a four-second beat, so the staircase starts on the beat), each walkway step by the walkway itself (the next step waits until the previous piece has landed and `walkway` has advanced), and the return doors by `last_ready`. A wait that is already over is not repeated, so a route that runs early or late meets the same machinery as one that runs on time. Easy has no flipping corridor (its four platforms carry the not-on-Easy flag and a row of static pads stands in their place), so there the route plans its jumps from pad to pad.
- **It is resource-aware.** It takes the pickups that lie off the way: the Vial of Will and the essence in the first hall and the essence under the arrival floor of the puzzle room (each restores Sanity, Will or both, only when the bar is not full). It descends to the levers by the open stairs from the arrival floor, a fall of a few units, where walking off the pit's edge costs 25 Sanity. It fights from the wide floors (the lever room, the middle and the east end of the arrival floor, the bridge) with `Route::clear`: Cards while there is Will to pay for them, the Blade otherwise, and then it walks over what the enemies dropped (a drop restores Sanity and Will alike). A perched Boojum only wakes when Alice comes within 1,200 units of it and from the middle of the arrival floor the far ones are still asleep, so the route also stands at the floor's east end, at the foot of the walkway, and fights what that wakes there, instead of meeting it on the walkway; and the Diamond in the room north of the bridge, which shoots as soon as Alice is in its sight and whose drop pays for the rest of that fight, is shot first with the Blade (`snipe`) when the Will for Cards is gone. It does not stop to fight on the walkway or the stairs (the weapons still fire at what is awake while it walks, as in every recorded route), fails the route if a fight ends in a portal (a fall, or a chase after a drop: `secure` checks), re-plans its way with `navigate` after a fight instead of walking a straight line from wherever the last shove left it, and waits for a Boojum that is still coming before it climbs or boards.

The first hall's club guards are not fought at arm's length (each hit costs 12 Sanity): they are shot down with Cards as they follow, as recorded.

Reviewed deviations and limits:

- The arch-room pit still kills on Normal and Easy (DG-7: difficulty bits are applied to registry maps only). The route never falls in.
- The musical-lever puzzle can only be solved by ear (a fidelity and accessibility follow-up). The route knows the order.
- At Hard the chain reaches Beyond the Wall with 20 Sanity and 1 Will, and the route passes there, with the narrowest margin of the three: the Blade and the drops pay for the fights (Hard's Cards weigh 0.7 and it places a third Boojum, `get_booj1`), the whole leg spends 18 combat damage and it leaves with 91 Sanity, but a run of perturbations (0 to 600 idle ticks added at five places of the leg, a temporary hook that is not part of the source) passed at Normal and at Easy every time and at Hard 9 times of 14. When an earlier visit changes what it hands over, Hard is the first run to check.
- Route timing values in ticks depend on the fights: the numbers above are what the final build prints.

Native captures confirm the levers, bridge, raised room and shuffled doors without missing images. A native F5 save, separate-process restart and F9 restore pass in `private/beyond-native-saves`. The hidden desktop had no audio device, so this run verifies visuals/save loading rather than audible playback. Original note/voice assets decode successfully. The current source passes 476 unit tests, strict Clippy and formatting checks.
