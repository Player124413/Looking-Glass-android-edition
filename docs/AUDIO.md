# Gameplay audio — version 0.32.0

Audio is played from the user's read-only PK3 archives through the Rust Rodio/CPAL output path. No original executable, Miles DLL or script is executed. No game sounds are included in the source package.

## Playback and events

Gameplay sound requests now resolve through the asset mount instead of silently disappearing when absent from a fixed preload list. Common sounds are preloaded; additional level sounds decode when first requested. Missing requests produce a diagnostic once per level. Decoding remains bounded at eight million interleaved samples per clip and 32 million cached mono samples. At the cache limit, a requested clip plays transiently without growing the cache. At most 32 ordinary one-shots, one Alice reaction voice and 32 managed loops can be active; active sources keep their own shared PCM references.

The implemented Knife, Cards, Mallet, Dice and Pocket Watch actions retain their existing animation-frame attack cues. Mallet melee, card impacts and mallet-ball impacts now resolve their appropriate flesh sounds; thrown-weapon impacts originate at their contact positions. Cards and mallet balls have moving flight loops. Implemented card guards, Boojums and Ladybugs emit positional alert/attack sounds, and hit/death reactions use the struck actor's location. The Duchess's previously uncached attacks, pain, death, pig explosions and defeat-sequence sounds now play; her attacks, explosions and timed defeat cues have world positions.

Alice's client `sound` commands are read as data from the TIKI model and matched by named animation performance. Performances sharing a frame file keep separate commands: ordinary `run_nowep` must not inherit the cry from `run_jump`. The controller explicitly maps its frame-file names to the swimming, climbing and death aliases. Running footfalls, swimming strokes, ledge climbs, rope climbing and death cues follow actual animation-frame crossings, including variable playback rate and loop wrap. Pausing and restoring an animation do not replay already crossed frames. Walking retains the accepted-distance footstep fallback because its currently used clips have no authored sound commands. Immediate jump and landing contacts remain controller-driven so they are not duplicated by animation events. Water entry, submerging, surfacing/gasping and leaving water have separate sounds, with an owned swimming loop. Death no longer pauses the entire audio system before its animation can sound.

Health feedback owns pain cries, preventing duplication from the cosmetic hurt animation. Continuous damage lets that animation finish instead of restarting its first frame. Pain, choking, surfacing and death use one exclusive reaction voice; repeats wait for the decoded clip duration plus 0.2 seconds, with a minimum 1.2-second interval. Choking replaces pain, surfacing interrupts choking, and death takes priority. Drowning damage does not insert ordinary pain cries between choking contacts. Normal pain uses the original animation's 0.7 volume. Pause freezes reaction timing; revival/map/save load clears old reactions. Damage rates and save formats are unchanged.

Original scripted mover sounds now follow implemented world clocks:

- Village roller: its two rolling cues at the two movement strokes.
- First school: falling-bookcase start and impact, plus the spiral lift's moving loop.
- Pandemonium: minecart lift start, lift/travel loops and landing splash. Loops stop when their phase ends or is skipped.

Moving loops follow object positions. Frozen mover clocks suppress their loops. Load/restore reconstructs current loops and seeds one-shot observers without replaying earlier impacts; no save-format change is required. Other interaction sounds already emitted by level logic, including doors, levers and quest objects, use the same resolver.

## Music, ambience and mixing

The `.mus` parser reads every named cue with its own path, volume, fade and loop setting. Most maps contain only `normal`; Funhouse and the Queen's lair also declare `suspense`. Level changes now crossfade outgoing/incoming tracks. At most two outgoing streams are retained, and fade clocks respect pause/mute. Tower3 uses `hedge1.mus`, matching its map's explicit soundtrack declaration. Dialogue reduces music while its dedicated voice stream is active.

The listener follows the actual viewing camera, including scripted camera shots. Ambient speakers and positional effects use equal-power stereo panning and distance falloff. Solid geometry, including current dynamic collision, attenuates and low-pass filters obstructed sources. Camera submersion filters world effects. Six short listener probes estimate enclosure for restrained stereo early reflections. These are independently implemented approximations, not a reproduction of the original Miles/EAX acoustics or portal propagation.

Active/random speakers come from `.snd` and BSP metadata, with exact duplicates removed. Inactive trigger speakers remain quiet unless supported gameplay explicitly activates their corresponding sound. Two obsolete build-directory ambience references map to exact existing filenames before duplicate detection. For those migrated BSP references only, a matching sound-manager source within 128 units takes precedence. This removes the doubled Village entrance forcefield (113 units apart), preserving its authored position, volume and radius, and all 19 other Village sources. Nearby ordinary speakers remain independent. The final mix has a soft knee above 0.8 and stays below 0.98 full scale, preserving quiet signals while limiting simultaneous loud transients. Music and dialogue are kept out of the world-effects room filter.

## Controls and restoration

Esc → Settings → Sound and F3 retain the existing original-art settings UI; M toggles mute. Music/effects settings persist in the selected local settings directory. Mute preserves stream positions, and pause freezes sink playback and fade clocks. Map changes clear old world sources while music fades away. Output-device failure leaves gameplay and subtitles working. Device reconnection still requires restarting the application.

Animation and machinery clocks restore from existing campaign saves. Transient one-shots are deliberately not serialized. Music currently restarts its normal cue with a fade on map/save load rather than restoring an exact MP3 sample position. Unsupported campaign scenes still need their level-owned music trigger events: parsing and the output test cover Funhouse's alternate cue, but do not constitute a restored Tweedle or Queen encounter. Exhaustive cinematic/NPC TIKI event parity, material-specific walking footfalls and exact original acoustic/mix matching remain future fidelity work.
